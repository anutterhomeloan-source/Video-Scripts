## Hook (Straight, personal)
If I could go back and buy my first home all over again...

these are the five things I would NOT skip.

## Branding
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem / Empathy
Here's the thing — most first-time buyers save money in exactly the wrong spots.

I get it. Buying a home is already expensive. You're looking for anywhere to cut corners.

But some things? They're not the place to save.

## Answer

VISUAL: Checklist graphic, 5 items — line 1 revealed, 2–5 blurred/dashed (first-home-again-checklist-v1.png)

Number one. The home inspection.

I don't care how competitive the market is. I don't care how badly you want the house. Get the inspection.

It's the one thing standing between you and finding out about the roof after you already own it.

VISUAL: Checklist updates — lines 1 & 2 revealed, 3–5 blurred (first-home-again-checklist-v2.png)

Number two. A realtor who's actually in your corner.

Not just someone who unlocks doors. Someone who negotiates for you, tells you the truth, and isn't afraid to say "walk away."

VISUAL: Checklist updates — lines 1–3 revealed, 4–5 blurred (first-home-again-checklist-v3.png)

Number three. A loan officer who actually explains things.

Not just someone who sends you a rate and disappears. Someone who walks you through every number on that page, so you're not signing blind.

VISUAL: Checklist updates — lines 1–4 revealed, 5 blurred (first-home-again-checklist-v4.png)

Number four. Cash left over after closing.

Don't drain every single dollar into your down payment. Keep a cushion. Because the day you close is usually the day the real expenses start.

VISUAL: Checklist complete — all 5 revealed (first-home-again-checklist-v5.png)

Number five. A home warranty.

It's not expensive, and it covers you when the water heater decides it's done — three months in.

## Change
So if you're buying your first home, don't ask "what can I skip to save a few hundred bucks."

Ask "what actually protects me on the other side of this."

## End Result
Those five things? They're the difference between buying a home you're proud of...

and buying a home that quietly stresses you out for the next two years.
