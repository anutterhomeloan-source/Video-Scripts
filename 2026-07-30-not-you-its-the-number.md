# It's Not You, It's the Number

- **Type:** Reenactment (2 parts)
- **Status:** Scripted
- **Date approved:** 2026-07-30
- **CTA:** None
- **Image asset:** None
- **Need:** Certainty (avoid pain) + Contribution (explains the "why" behind a denial people don't understand)

*Jordan plays both roles: **DANA** (borrower, upset after being told she doesn't qualify) and **JORDAN** (calm, direct LO). Each part runs 60–90 seconds.*

---

## PART 1 — "You're supposed to make this work"

**DANA:** *(heated, slamming a folder down)* Okay, I need you to explain this to me right now.

**JORDAN:** Dana, hey — I know you're upset. Talk to me.

**DANA:** You told me I could get a house. I applied. I gave you everything you asked for.

**JORDAN:** I did pull your full file, yeah.

**DANA:** So why did I just get a "does not qualify"?

**JORDAN:** Because right now, you've got fifty-six thousand dollars in collections.

**DANA:** Okay... and?

**JORDAN:** Across fifteen different accounts.

**DANA:** So? I never said I was perfect.

**JORDAN:** And your credit score is sitting at five hundred.

**DANA:** *(louder)* So this is on you? You're supposed to make this work!

**JORDAN:** Dana — I'm not the one who set the guidelines. I don't get to waive them.

**DANA:** Then what am I even paying you for?

**JORDAN:** You're not paying me to approve you. You're paying me to tell you the truth. And the truth is — this isn't about me not trying.

**DANA:** Then what is it about?

**JORDAN:** It's about the number. Give me two minutes, and I'll show you exactly why five hundred — and fifty-six thousand in collections — is the actual wall here.

Not me.

**TEXT OVERLAY (end card):** *Part 2 — the number that actually broke it.*

---

## PART 2 — "It's not you, it's the number"

**DANA:** *(arms crossed)* Fine. Two minutes. Go.

**JORDAN:** Five-eighty. That's the floor for most loans. Some sit at six-twenty. You're at five hundred.

**DANA:** Okay...

**JORDAN:** You said this was on me. It's not. It's on this number. Doesn't matter how much you make — if you're under that floor, no lender in the country can approve you. Not me. Not anybody.

**DANA:** But that's just a number. I still make good money.

**JORDAN:** I hear you. But underwriting doesn't just look at income. It looks at your history of paying people back.

Fifty-six thousand dollars, unpaid, across fifteen accounts — that's fifteen separate stories of "this didn't get paid." That's what tanked the score.

**DANA:** *(quieter)* I didn't realize it added up like that.

**JORDAN:** Most people don't. It's not one big mistake. It's fifteen small ones that piled up.

**DANA:** So... is this it? Am I just stuck?

**JORDAN:** No. It's not permanent. But it's not a "next week" fix either. We start knocking out the right collections, in the right order — and that score starts climbing.

**DANA:** *(sighs)* I'm sorry I came in yelling.

**JORDAN:** You're not the first, and you won't be the last. This isn't personal, Dana.

It's math.

And math can change.

---

## PEACE
- **Problem:** She applied, expected an approval, and instead got denied — and directs her anger at Jordan.
- **Empathy:** A denial letter with no explanation feels personal, even when it isn't.
- **Answer:** Lenders have a hard minimum credit score regardless of income, and $56K across 15 collections is what dragged the score to 500.
- **Change:** Start paying down/negotiating the right collections in the right order to rebuild the score.
- **End Result:** She understands it was never personal — it's math, and math can change.
