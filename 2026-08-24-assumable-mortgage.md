## Hook
"Selling your house means giving up your rate forever."
That's what everyone assumes.
It's not always true.

## Branding
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem
Right now, a lot of sellers are stuck.
They bought when rates were under 3%.
Selling means trading that rate for something twice as high.
So a lot of great homes just... aren't hitting the market.

## Empathy
And if you're a buyer, that feels like bad luck you can't do anything about.

## Answer
Here's the part almost nobody talks about.
Some loans are assumable.
VA loans. FHA loans.
That means a qualified buyer can literally take over the seller's exact loan — same rate, same term, same balance.

VISUAL: Text overlay — "VA & FHA loans can be assumable"

## Change (example)
Let's say a seller has a $320,000 loan at 2.75%.
Home is worth $420,000 now.
The buyer doesn't get a new 6.5% loan on the full price.
They take over that same $320,000 balance at 2.75% — and just need to cover the $100,000 difference, usually with a second loan or cash.

VISUAL: Number overlay — "$320K balance @ 2.75% assumed" / "+$100K gap covered separately"

## End Result
It's not a fit for every deal.
But for the right buyer, it's a real way into a rate that doesn't exist anymore.
