## Hook
Negotiating the seller down $10,000 feels like the win.
That's the number you can see.
It's usually not the bigger one.

## Branding
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem
Buyers negotiate hard on price because it's the number right there in the contract. It feels like control.

## Empathy
And to be fair — a lower price is a real win. I'm not saying don't ask.

## Answer
But there's another option that uses that exact same seller credit differently.
It's called a 2-1 buydown.
The seller's money temporarily buys your rate down — 2% lower in year one, 1% lower in year two.

VISUAL: 2-line overlay — "Year 1: rate -2%" / "Year 2: rate -1%"

## Change (example)
Let's say the seller agrees to a $10,000 credit either way.
Applied to price, that might save you around $55 a month.
Applied to a 2-1 buydown on that same loan, it can save you closer to $300 a month in year one.

VISUAL: Number overlay — "$10K to price: ~$55/mo" / "$10K to buydown: ~$300/mo (yr. 1)" (buydown-comparison-card.png)

## End Result
Same seller concession. Completely different impact on your monthly budget.

## Stance
So before you assume asking for a lower price is automatically the smarter ask — it's not, not always. Run the numbers both ways first.
