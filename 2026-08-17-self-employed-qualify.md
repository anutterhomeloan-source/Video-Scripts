## Hook
"I'm self-employed, I probably can't get approved."

I hear that sentence at least once a week.

And it's usually not true.

## Branding
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem
A few months ago I had a client — runs his own landscaping business, five years in, doing great.

He went to a big bank first.

They looked at his tax returns and basically told him, "your income's too complicated, we can't work with this."

## Empathy
And I get why that's scary to hear.

Self-employed income does look different on paper.

You write off expenses to save on taxes — which is smart — but it can make your income look smaller than it actually is.

## Answer
Here's what a lot of people don't know.

There's more than one way to qualify.

We can average two years of tax returns and add back things like depreciation.

We can qualify off bank statements instead of tax returns.

For rental properties, there's even a loan based on the property's rental income — not your personal income at all.

VISUAL: Checklist overlay — "2-yr tax return average / bank statements / rental income (DSCR)"

## Change (example)
For my landscaping client, we used his two-year average, plus his add-backs.

On paper, his net income looked like $48,000.

With add-backs, we qualified him closer to $80,000.

He closed on his house two months later.

VISUAL: Number overlay — "$48K on paper → $80K qualifying income"

## End Result
Self-employed doesn't mean unqualifiable.

It just means you need someone who knows how to read your income the right way.
