## Hook

Pre-approved means you're good to go, right?
…Not if you got that letter before rates moved this much.
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

A pre-approval letter is based on your numbers on the day it was written.
Your income. Your debts. Your credit.
And the rate at that time.
Over the last week, rates had their biggest jump in four years.
Realtor.com's senior economist said this climb has added more than $200 a month to the payment on a median-priced home.
VISUAL: Text call-out "+$200/month" — Realtor.com via Fox Business, Oct 1


## Empathy

And here's the frustrating part.
You did the responsible thing.
You got pre-approved early.
That was the right move.

## Answer

But a pre-approval isn't a forever pass.
Most are good for about 90 days.
And even inside that window, if rates moved, what you can afford may have moved too.
Here's what I mean.
The same payment that gets you a $400,000 loan at 7%?
At 7.5%, it only gets you about $380,000.
Same budget. Twenty thousand dollars less house.
VISUAL: Side by side: 7.0% → $400,000 loan vs 7.5% → ~$380,000 loan · "same $2,661/month payment"

And I've seen what happens next.
Someone writes an offer on an old letter.
The numbers don't hold up at the new rate.
And now they're scrambling in the middle of a contract.

## Change

So before you write your next offer:
Call your lender.
Get your numbers re-run at today's rate.
Make sure your letter matches the house you're about to offer on, and that it's not about to expire.
