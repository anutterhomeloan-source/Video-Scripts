# Waiving the Inspection Doesn't Make You a Stronger Buyer

**Type:** Talking Head
**Status:** Scripted
**Date approved:** 2026-09-14
**CTA:** Save this
**Image asset:** None

---

**Hook**
Waiving your inspection is what serious buyers do to win the house. That's what everyone tells you in this market. And it's exactly how people end up owning a house that bankrupts them in repairs.

**Branding**
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help mortgages make sense.

**Proof**
I've watched this happen more than once. Buyer waives the inspection to win a bidding war. Moves in a few weeks later. Finds out the HVAC system is dead. Or the roof's got two years left in it. And now that's coming out of their pocket — not the seller's.

**Distinction**
I'm not talking about walking away from a great house over a leaky faucet or a cracked tile. I'm talking about the fifteen-thousand-dollar surprise nobody budgeted for.

VISUAL: Text overlay — "$15,000 surprise" on the reveal line

**Answer**
Here's the thing — waiving inspection completely isn't your only option to stay competitive. You can do an inspection for information only. You still get eyes on the house, you just aren't using it to renegotiate. You can shorten the inspection window instead of removing it. Or in some markets, get a pre-inspection before you even write the offer.

VISUAL: Checklist overlay — "3 options besides waiving completely: info-only, shortened window, pre-inspection"

**Change**
Ask your agent about these before you default to waiving it completely. Because winning the house isn't the win if you can't afford what's behind the walls.

**End Result**
Waiving your inspection was never what made you a stronger buyer. Being informed is.

**CTA**
Save this in case you're about to write an offer in a bidding war.
