## Hook

"Marry the house, date the rate."  
You've heard it. Maybe from an agent. Maybe from a lender.  
…And right now, it's one of the most expensive things anybody can tell you.  
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.  

## Problem

Here's how it goes.  
You find the house. The payment's a stretch.  
And somebody says, "Don't worry. You'll just refinance when rates come down."  
So you buy at the very top of your budget…  
counting on a refinance that hasn't happened yet.  

> VISUAL: text overlay "Marry the house, date the rate," then a strike-through

## Empathy

And I get why it sounds good.  
It sounds smart. It sounds like a plan.  

## Answer

But a refinance isn't a promise.  
It's a whole new loan.  
You have to qualify all over again.  
You need enough equity, and Redfin just reported home prices are basically flat from a year ago.  
And you pay closing costs again. Usually thousands of dollars.  
And nobody can tell you when rates will move, or how much.  
Not me. Not anybody.  

> VISUAL: Freddie Mac screenshot, 30-year at 7.24% (Oct 29) + Redfin "−0.1%" headline

## Stance

So here's my take.  
If the payment only works after a refinance…  
the payment doesn't work.  

## Change

Buy at a payment you could live with for five years, at today's rate.  
If that number is lower than what you got approved for? Good.  
That's your real number.  
And if rates come down later? Great.  
That's a bonus. Not the plan.  

> VISUAL: two-line overlay "The plan: a payment you can hold today / The bonus: a refinance later"

## End Result + CTA

Because then, if rates drop… you win.  
And if they don't?  
You're still fine.  
So. Don't buy a house right now if you're counting on a refinance to afford it.  
If that's not you, we should probably talk.  
Comment "not me."
