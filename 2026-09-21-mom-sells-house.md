# Mom Sells It to Me for $1

**Type:** Reenactment
**Status:** Scripted
**Date approved:** 2026-09-21
**CTA:** None
**Image asset:** None
**Need:** Growth + Connection

*Illustrative scenario (not a specific real client). Characters: Jordan (loan officer), Jess (buyer), Mom (voice). Mechanic: a family dynamic — a below-market family sale structured as a gift of equity ($300K appraisal, $240K price, $60K gift = 80% LTV, no PMI).*

Rules per [Fannie Mae B3-4.3-05](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.3-05/gifts-equity): appraisal, signed gift letter, primary or second homes only. Gift tax: donor may need to file — refer to a tax pro.

---

## Part 1

**JESS:** Jordan, my mom's selling me the house I grew up in.

**JORDAN:** That's amazing! How much?

**JESS:** A dollar.

**JORDAN:** A dollar.

**JESS:** She wants me to have it. A friend said it's easy.

**JORDAN:** I love that for you. But a lender can't close on a dollar.

**JESS:** Why not? It's family!

**JESS:** She lived here for thirty years.

**JORDAN:** I know. That's exactly why it matters.

**JORDAN:** Because the loan is based on what the house is worth.

**JESS:** It's worth a dollar to her.

**JORDAN:** Aww. But not to an appraiser.

**JESS:** So Mom's plan doesn't work?

**JORDAN:** It's close. It just needs paperwork.

**JESS:** She's not even good at texting.

**JORDAN:** We'll make it easy for her.

**JORDAN:** Let me ask you something. If it's worth three hundred thousand and she sells it to you for two-forty, what's the difference?

**JESS:** Sixty thousand.

**JORDAN:** That's a gift of equity.

**JESS:** A gift of what?

**JORDAN:** Equity. The part of the house you don't owe on.

**JORDAN:** She's giving you sixty thousand dollars in the house.

**JESS:** Oh my gosh. Wait. Does my brother know?

**JORDAN:** He doesn't know?

**JESS:** He is going to lose it.

## Part 2

**JESS:** My brother texted. He's not happy.

**JORDAN:** We can't fix the sibling group chat. But we can make the paper clean.

**JESS:** Fine. What do we need?

**JORDAN:** One, an appraisal. That sets the value.

**JESS:** Okay.

**JORDAN:** Two, a gift letter signed by your mom. It states the amount and that she doesn't expect repayment.

**JESS:** She'll sign anything if there's a pen next to it.

**JORDAN:** Three, the sale price on the contract is real. Two-forty. Not a dollar.

**JESS:** And the sixty thousand?

**JORDAN:** It shows on the closing disclosure as a credit toward your down payment.

**JESS:** So that's twenty percent down.

**JORDAN:** On a three hundred thousand appraisal? Yes. Which means no PMI.

**JESS:** No PMI?!

**JORDAN:** None. Because you're at eighty percent.

**JORDAN:** Your mom may also have a gift tax form to file. Talk to a tax pro.

**JESS:** I'll tell her.

**JESS:** Anything else?

**JORDAN:** Call your brother. Tell him it's documented.

**JESS:** Okay. I'll call him.

*(closing table)*

**MOM:** *(on phone)* Did it work?

**JESS:** Mom. You just gave me sixty thousand dollars.

**MOM:** I just wanted you to have the house.

**JORDAN:** Welcome home.

## Captions

Part 1 — "Mom says she'll sell me the house for a dollar." A lender can't close on that, but a below-market family sale can work as a gift of equity. It just needs an appraisal and real paperwork. Would you buy your childhood home?

Part 2 — Here's how a family sale can turn into a down payment. The appraisal sets the value, a gift letter documents the gift, and the difference between value and price counts toward your equity. Talk to a tax pro about gift tax.
