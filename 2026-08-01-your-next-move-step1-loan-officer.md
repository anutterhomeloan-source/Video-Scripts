## Script

You can't always tell who's reliable...

until something goes wrong.

And by then, you might already be scrambling.

This is step one of nine in Your Next Move — everything nobody tells you before you're under contract.

VISUAL: Your Next Move board graphic, Step 1 (Loan Officer) highlighted — your-next-move-step1.png

Ten days before closing, my phone rings.

It's a client I hadn't talked to in six weeks.

She'd shopped around when we first talked. Compared me to a couple other lenders. Ended up going with a bigger name — one of those big call-center lenders, the kind with the jingles and the commercials. Made sense on paper. I got it. I really did.

Then ten days before she was supposed to close, she calls me. Panicking.

And it wasn't just her deal. It was a chain. She's buying this house, but the sellers can't buy their next house until she closes. One phone call, and I already knew — if she doesn't close on time, three families are stuck.

Turns out that lender never even collected her full paperwork up front. Then they miscalculated her numbers. Then they just... went quiet. Ten days out.

So we scrambled. We got it done. She closed on time.

But I still think about that call.

Because that's exactly it. She couldn't tell the lender wasn't reliable — not until something already went wrong. And by then, she was scrambling, with three families' closings hanging on ten days.

That's why step one isn't the house. It's not even the realtor.

It's finding the loan officer who actually answers your questions. Who picks up. Who you trust before you've even signed a single piece of paper.

Because you won't know if they're reliable... until you need them to be.

See you for step two — where I tell you why the number a lender approves you for might not be the number you should actually spend.
