## Part 1

*Jordan's office. Marcus sits down, relaxed, like he's already got this figured out.*

**MARCUS:** Jordan, hey — good news. I actually already bought the house.

**JORDAN:** Wait — the one on Maple? We never ran your numbers.

**MARCUS:** We didn't need to. Dave — the seller — he's letting me just take over his payments. Same house, same loan, way lower rate than anything you quoted me.

**JORDAN:** Okay... take over the payments how? Did you go through his lender?

**MARCUS:** No, we just worked it out between us. Handshake deal. He hands me the keys, I send him the mortgage payment every month instead of him.

**JORDAN:** Marcus. Is your name on the loan anywhere? On the title?

**MARCUS:** Not yet. We're gonna get to the paperwork eventually. It's fine, Dave's a good guy.

**JORDAN:** I need you to stop moving for a second and let me ask you something.

**MARCUS:** ...Okay, that tone is not making me feel good.

**JORDAN:** Does Dave's mortgage have a "due on sale" clause? Have you even seen his loan documents?

**MARCUS:** I mean — probably? Most loans do, right? Why does that matter, we're not selling, I'm just paying it.

**JORDAN:** Marcus, transferring the house to you is a sale. That clause means his lender can call the entire loan due, in full, the second they find out.

## Part 2

*Two days later.*

**JORDAN:** Okay, I pulled up how due-on-sale actually works, and I need you to really hear this.

**MARCUS:** Hit me.

**JORDAN:** The second the deed changes hands — even informally — Dave's lender has the legal right to demand the entire remaining balance immediately. Not a payment plan. The whole thing, all at once.

**MARCUS:** But we're not telling them. How would they even find out?

**JORDAN:** Insurance changes. Property records update. Dave refinances something else and it shows up. It doesn't take much — and lenders check.

**MARCUS:** Okay, but let's say they never find out. Then it's fine, right?

**JORDAN:** Even then — your name isn't on the title correctly, so you don't legally, fully own this house the way you think you do. If Dave has a lien, a divorce, a lawsuit — that house is still tangled up in his name, not just his loan.

**MARCUS:** Jordan, I already gave him first and last month's "payment."

**JORDAN:** I know. And I'm telling you now because there's still a way to fix this the right way — but you have to stop sending him money until we do.

**MARCUS:** ...I really thought I found the loophole.

**JORDAN:** I get why it looked like one.

## Part 3

*One week later.*

**MARCUS:** So... Dave's lender found out. They sent him a letter. They're calling the whole loan due in 30 days.

**JORDAN:** How'd they catch it?

**MARCUS:** He switched the homeowners insurance to my name so I'd stop bugging him about it. That's apparently all it took.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Here's what we do. Was Dave's original loan a VA or FHA loan, by any chance?

**MARCUS:** ...I think FHA? Why?

**JORDAN:** Because if it is, there's an actual legal path for exactly what you two were trying to do. It's called a loan assumption. You qualify with his lender, they approve you, the loan transfers into your name for real — same rate Dave has, fully legal, title actually changes hands.

**MARCUS:** That exists? Why didn't we just do that from the beginning?

**JORDAN:** Because nobody told you it existed, so you two invented your own version of it. The real process takes longer and it's more paperwork. But it's the difference between owning that house and owing $280,000 by next month with nothing to show for it.

**MARCUS:** I am never handshake-deal-ing a house again.

**JORDAN:** This is exactly why I say it every time — before you get creative with someone else's mortgage, call me first.
