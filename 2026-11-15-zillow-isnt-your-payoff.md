## Hook

So a seller at my company just found out, a couple of weeks before closing, that they owed twenty-five thousand dollars more on their house than they thought.

And now that closing is getting pushed, because they have to come up with that money before they can sell.

And if you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter and I help make mortgages make sense.


## Problem

So here's why this happens. Most people figure out what they owe from their last statement, or they look at Zillow, subtract their loan balance, and call that their equity.


## Empathy

And honestly, that's what almost everybody does. Nobody ever tells you that your payoff is a different number.


## Answer

Your payoff is the exact amount it takes to close out your loan on a specific day, and it can be higher than you think for a few reasons.

First, interest. Mortgage interest is generally paid behind, so there's usually some built up that isn't showing in your balance yet.

Second, something you forgot about, like a home equity line you opened years ago, or a second mortgage.

Third, if you ever paused payments, like a forbearance during COVID, some of those missed payments may have been set aside to be paid back when you sell or refinance.

And fourth, liens. Things like unpaid taxes or a judgment can attach to the house, and those have to be paid off at closing too.

> VISUAL: Text call-outs, one per point: "INTEREST" · "FORGOT A HELOC?" · "FORBEARANCE" · "LIENS"


## Change

So here's what I'd do. Before you list your house, call your servicer and ask for a payoff statement. They have to give you one, usually within about a week, and it's the real number.


## Stance

So here's my take. Nobody should put their house on the market without seeing their payoff in writing.


## End Result + CTA

Because finding out about twenty-five thousand dollars before you sign a listing agreement is a whole lot better than finding out a month before closing. So save this, in case you're thinking about selling next year.
