## Hook

The White House wants to cancel your PMI automatically.  
But here's the part the headline skips.  
You might be able to cancel it yourself.  
Like… now.  
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.  

## Problem

Last week, the president signed an executive order on housing costs.  
One piece of it tells the agency over Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to test a program.  
That program would cancel your mortgage insurance automatically…  
once you've built enough equity based on what your home is worth today.  
VISUAL: HousingWire headline screenshot (Oct 22) with the PMI line highlighted

## What People Have Wrong

Sounds great, right?  
But this is a test program. It's not a law.  
And reports say it would only cover new loans, the ones made after it launches.  
So if you already have a mortgage…  
this probably isn't coming to save you.  

## Empathy

And if you didn't know you could drop PMI on your own?  
You're not alone.  
Nobody calls you to tell you.  

## Answer

On most conventional loans, once you hit 20% equity, you can ask your servicer to remove it.  
And it comes off automatically a little after that, on your original schedule.  
And if your home's value went up?  
You might get there faster than you think.  
VISUAL: Simple bar: "Ask to remove: 20% equity" → "Comes off on its own: 22%"
Now, if you have an FHA loan, that works differently. That's its own conversation.  

## Change

So here's what I'd do.  
Pull up your mortgage statement. Find your balance.  
Look up what your home's worth.  
If you're close to 20%, call your servicer and ask what they need to take it off.  

## Stance

Here's my take.  
I actually like this order.  
But don't wait on a pilot program for money you can already ask for.  

## Close + CTA

The cheapest PMI is the one you stop paying.  
So tell me. Are you still paying PMI? Yes or no. Drop it in the comments.
