## Hook
You locked in a fixed rate.

So why did your payment just go up $250 a month?

## Branding
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem / Empathy
Every year I get this call.

"Jordan, my rate didn't change. Why is my payment different?"

And I get it — "fixed rate" sounds like everything's locked. Forever.

But that's not actually what fixed means.

## Answer
Your payment has two parts.

Principal and interest — that part really is fixed. It never moves.

But taxes and insurance? Those live in your payment too, through something called escrow.

And those two things can change every single year.

VISUAL: 2-line overlay — "Principal & Interest: locked forever" / "Taxes & Insurance: can change every year"

## Change (example)
Let's say your payment was $2,610.

Your county reassesses your home. Property taxes go up.

Your homeowners insurance renews higher.

Now that same fixed-rate loan has a payment of $2,860.

Your rate didn't move an inch. Your escrow did.

VISUAL: Number overlay — "$2,610 → $2,860 · same rate, new escrow"

So here's what I want you to do.

You'll get an escrow analysis in the mail every year — actually open it.

Budget a little cushion for taxes and insurance to creep up.

And if the jump feels bigger than it should, call your lender and ask why before you panic.

## End Result
A fixed rate is still one of the smartest things you can lock in.

You just need to know what it actually protects — and what it doesn't.
