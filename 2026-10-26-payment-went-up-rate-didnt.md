## Hook

In case nobody ever explained this to you…  
your mortgage payment can go up.  
Even with a fixed rate.  
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.  

## Problem

You open your statement and your payment jumped two hundred dollars.  
And you're like… I locked my rate. What happened?  

## Empathy

Nobody explains this at closing.  
You're signing a hundred pages. You're just trying to get the keys.  

## Answer

Your payment is really two parts.  
There's the loan part. Principal and interest. That's locked.  
And there's the house part. Property taxes and homeowners insurance.  
That's your escrow. And that part can change every year.  
VISUAL: Payment split: "Locked: principal + interest" vs "Can move: taxes + insurance"
Once a year, your servicer reviews it.  
If your taxes or insurance went up, your payment goes up.  
And if they came up short last year, they can add that in too.  

## Change

So here's what to do.  
One. Actually read your escrow statement when it comes.  
Two. If your property assessment jumped, you can appeal it.  
Here in Georgia, you get 45 days from your assessment notice. Those come out in the spring, so watch for it.  
Three. Shop your homeowners insurance. Every year.  
VISUAL: 3-item checklist overlay

## Stance + Close

Fixed rate doesn't mean fixed payment.  
And you deserve to know that before closing, not after.  
Save this for when your escrow statement shows up.  
That's the straight answer you wish your lender gave.
