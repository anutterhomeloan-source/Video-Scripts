*Phil Jones (9/29 mastermind): answer "what's the rate?" with slow questions ("how long have you been thinking about it?") and raise the floor by walking the worst case. New mechanic: couple disagreement + fear of giving up a low rate (no underwriting surprise, no lender switch). Characters: Jordan, Chris, Dana (on speaker). Example math: home ~$430K, owe $270K, ~7% selling costs → ~$130K net; buy $575K, 20% down, 7%; after 3 yrs owe ~$445K; 10% drop → ~$518K, still positive after selling costs.*

---

## Part 1


*[Jordan at the desk. Phone on speaker. Chris and Dana calling together.]*

**CHRIS:** Hey, Jordan? Quick question. We just need your rate. That's it.

**JORDAN:** Hi! Sure. Rate for what? Buying? Refinancing?

**DANA:** Buying. We want to move. We need a bigger place.

**CHRIS:** Maybe. Maybe we want to move.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Do you two own right now?

**CHRIS:** Yeah. And we have a 2.75 rate.

**JORDAN:** Ooh. That's a good one.

**CHRIS:** It's a great one. So what's yours? Just give us the number.

**JORDAN:** I will. Let me ask you one thing first. How long have you two been thinking about moving?

**DANA:** Four years.

**CHRIS:** Two.

**DANA:** Four. Since the baby.

**CHRIS:** Two. Seriously.

**JORDAN:** Okay. So… a while.

**DANA:** Jordan, the kids share a room. Our office is the laundry room. I take work calls next to the dryer.

**CHRIS:** And if we sell, we lose the 2.75. Forever. Forever, Dana.

**JORDAN:** That's fair. That's a real thing to give up.

**CHRIS:** So we're waiting until rates come down.

**DANA:** That's what you said last year.

**CHRIS:** Jordan. Just tell us the rate.

**JORDAN:** Chris. Can I ask you the real question? What happens if you buy, and three years from now, you have to sell?

**CHRIS:** (long pause) That. That's exactly what keeps me up at night.

## Part 2


*[TEXT ON SCREEN: Part 2]*

**DANA:** So he's scared we buy, the house drops, and we lose everything.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Let's actually look at that. Not the dream. The worst case.

**CHRIS:** Fine.

**JORDAN:** When you sell your place, you'd walk away with about $130,000. You put most of that down on the new house. That's 20% down.

**CHRIS:** Okay.

**JORDAN:** Now say the worst happens. Prices drop 10%. And three years in, you have to sell.

**CHRIS:** See? That's what I'm talking about.

**JORDAN:** You'd still owe less than the house is worth. You'd sell, pay everything off, and walk away with money.

**CHRIS:** …Less money.

**JORDAN:** Less money. Not zero. And you don't owe the bank a dime.

**DANA:** So the worst case is… we're okay?

**JORDAN:** The worst case is you're annoyed. Not ruined.

**CHRIS:** And if rates drop?

**JORDAN:** Then we look at refinancing. And if they don't, you're fine, because we pick a payment you can live with before you ever make an offer.

**CHRIS:** Huh.

**DANA:** Four years, Chris.

**CHRIS:** Two and a half.

**JORDAN:** So. Do you still want to know the rate?

**CHRIS:** …Yeah. Now I actually do.

**JORDAN:** Good. Now it's the right question.


*[TEXT ON SCREEN: The rate wasn't the problem. Not knowing the worst case was.]*

## Captions (approved)
Part 1 — They called for a rate. What they really needed was an answer to the question keeping him up at night. Giving up a 2.75% rate is a big deal, and it's okay to be scared of it. But "just give me the number" doesn't help you decide anything. Which one are you, Chris or Dana?

Part 2 — When you put real money down, a 10% price drop usually doesn't wipe you out. It just shrinks what you walk away with. That's the math nobody runs when they're stuck on the rate. Would knowing your worst case change your mind?
