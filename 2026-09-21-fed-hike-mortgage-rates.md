**Real story:** The Fed raised rates 25 bps to 3.75%–4.00% on Sept 16, 2026, its first hike since 2023. Freddie Mac's 30-year average rose from 6.76% to 6.95% that week, but the 10-year Treasury fell about 6 bps to 4.94% the day after and mortgage rates eased slightly.

---

## Hook

The Fed raised interest rates this week.

So mortgage rates must be climbing because of it, right?

Not exactly.

They climbed before the Fed acted. And they eased after.

## Branding

If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

Freddie Mac's weekly average went from 6.76 to 6.95 percent.

The Fed hiked the next day, and the ten-year Treasury actually dropped about six basis points.

VISUAL: Source overlay, "Freddie Mac: 6.76% → 6.95%"

## Empathy

So if you thought the hike pushed rates up, that's what the headlines told you.

## Answer

Your mortgage rate doesn't follow the Fed.

It follows the ten-year Treasury.

The hike was mostly expected, so rates had already moved up.

Then the ten-year dropped, because investors felt the Fed was serious about inflation.

VISUAL: Text card, "Fed +0.25% → 10-yr −6 bps"

## Stance

Here's my take.

A Fed day is the worst day to make a rate decision.

The headline moves first, and the ten-year tells you the truth.

## Change

If you're about to lock, ask your lender about a float-down option.

That way, if rates drop after you lock, you're not stuck.

## End Result

You stop reacting to Fed headlines and start watching the number that matters.
