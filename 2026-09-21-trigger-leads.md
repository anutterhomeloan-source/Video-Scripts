## Hook

You check your mortgage rate, and then your phone rings.

Then it rings again.

A law was supposed to stop that in March.

## Branding

If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

Within hours of applying, lenders you never heard of were calling and texting, sometimes quoting your exact loan amount.

## Empathy

It felt invasive, and it was confusing.

How do they even know?

## Answer

Credit bureaus were selling your information as soon as a lender checked your credit.

They're called trigger leads.

Since March fifth, the Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act limits that, with a few exceptions, like the lender you're actually working with.

VISUAL: Card, "Effective March 5, 2026: Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act"

## Change

If you're still getting those calls, don't engage, and tell your loan officer.

Be careful with anyone who knows your loan details but isn't the lender you applied with.

## Stance

I think this law is overdue.

Shopping around shouldn't cost you a ringing phone.

## End Result

You get to compare lenders without being chased.
