## Hook (Misconception Flip)

Everyone says people won't move because they don't want to give up their 3% rate.
I think that's only half the story.
Because sometimes it's not the rate keeping them stuck.
It's what they did with the money it freed up.
VISUAL: Text overlay "It's not the rate. It's the lifestyle."

If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

I have a friend whose mortgage is $1,700 a month.
The house they want next? About $4,000.
On paper, they qualify.
But it feels impossible.
Because when the payment was low, life got a little roomier.
Two nicer cars. A few more trips.
It just filled the space.
VISUAL: Side-by-side "$1,700 now → $4,000 next", with "+2 car payments, +trips" under the $1,700

## Empathy + Receipts

And that's not a character flaw.
Economists have actually studied this.
When people's mortgage payment drops, one of the first things they spend it on is a car.
VISUAL: Screenshot of the NBER Digest article (see Screenshot sources)

Here in Atlanta, the typical payment in 2021 was about $1,650.
Last year? Over $2,700.
And yes, everything else went up too. That part's not on you.
VISUAL: Screenshots of the FOX 5 ($1,653) and WSB-TV ($2,712) articles

## Answer

But here's what nobody tells you.
At today's rates, every $770 car payment eats about $120,000 of buying power.
Two of them? That's almost a quarter million dollars of house.
VISUAL: Screenshot of the FOX 5 car-payment article, then calculator overlay "$770/mo = ~$120K less house · ×2 = ~$240K"

## Change

So if you want to move in the next year, do two things.
One, don't add a single new payment.
Two, do a payment rehearsal.
Save the difference between your payment now and your future one, every month, for six months.
For my friend, that's $2,300 a month.
If your budget handles it, you're ready, and you just built a cushion.
If it doesn't, you know exactly what has to change first.

## Stance + End Result

Here's my take.
The 3% rate isn't the golden handcuffs.
The lifestyle you built on top of it is.

## CTA (Discussion question)

So I want to know.
If you've got a low rate, what's really keeping you put? The rate, or the budget?
Tell me in the comments. It tells me a lot about where this market is actually stuck.

## Caption (approved)

Everyone blames the 3% rate for keeping people stuck. I think it's half the story. When mortgages got cheap in 2020–21, budgets quietly filled the gap with nicer cars, more trips and more subscriptions. At about 6.5%, a single $770 car payment costs roughly $120K of buying power. If you're thinking of moving, try a "payment rehearsal" first: save the difference for six months and see how it feels. Rate or budget, what's keeping you put? 👇
