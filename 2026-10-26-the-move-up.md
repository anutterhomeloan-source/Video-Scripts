## Part 1

**DANA:** Jordan! Okay. We're doing it. We're finally moving.  
**JORDAN:** Love that. Tell me what's going on.  
**DANA:** So we have the 3% rate, right? Our payment's like seventeen hundred.  
**JORDAN:** That's a great rate. Hold that thought.  
**DANA:** I know, I know. But we've got two kids sharing a room. My husband works from the dining table. We are out of space.  
**JORDAN:** Okay. That's a real reason. What are you looking at?  
**DANA:** There's a house in our same school district. Five fifty. Four bedrooms, a real office, a backyard.  
**JORDAN:** And you'd sell your current house?  
**DANA:** Yeah. We'd walk away with about a hundred and twenty thousand.  
**JORDAN:** Okay, that's a strong down payment. Let me pull everything up real quick.  
**DANA:** I've been looking at that house every night. I already picked out paint colors.  
**JORDAN:** Okay.  
**DANA:** "Okay" good? Or "okay" bad?  
**JORDAN:** Your credit is great. Your income is great.  
**DANA:** So how much can we get approved for?  
**JORDAN:** Before I answer that…  
**DANA:** What?  
**JORDAN:** Can I ask you about your cars?  
**DANA:** …Why are you asking about my cars?  

## Part 2

**DANA:** Okay, you pulled our credit and now you're asking about my cars. Why are we talking about my cars?  
**JORDAN:** Because right now you have two car payments. One's seven eighty. One's six ninety.  
**DANA:** Yeah, we upgraded both a couple years ago. We could afford it.  
**JORDAN:** You could. When your house payment dropped, that extra room got filled. That happens to almost everybody.  
**DANA:** But we pay them on time. Every month.  
**JORDAN:** You do. That's not the problem. Lenders count every one of those payments against your income. Plus there's a travel card with a four hundred dollar minimum.  
**DANA:** That's from Disney.  
**JORDAN:** I figured. So here's the honest version. With all three of those payments, that house puts you right at the edge.  
**DANA:** So we can't do it.  
**JORDAN:** I didn't say that. I said not with all three.  
**DANA:** What does that mean?  
**JORDAN:** It means you have options. Pay off one car with some of your sale money. Or trade it down before we apply. And clear out that card.  
**DANA:** Honestly? My husband loves that truck.  
**JORDAN:** Does he love it more than an office with a door?  
**DANA:** …I'll ask him.  
**JORDAN:** Ask him tonight. And before we sign anything, do a practice run. Set aside the difference in payments for a few months. See how it feels. You're not just buying the house. You're buying the life inside it.  

## CAPTIONS

Caption 1: Part 1 — She has a 3% rate, a strong down payment, and great credit. So why did her loan officer ask about her cars? Moving up gets tricky when the payment that used to feel small quietly filled up with other things. Part 2 is coming. Would you have guessed what Jordan found?
Caption 2: Part 2 — Two car payments and a travel card were eating the room a bigger house needed. The fix wasn't giving up on moving. It was choosing what to clear first, then test-driving the new payment for a few months before signing. Would you give up a car for a bigger house?
