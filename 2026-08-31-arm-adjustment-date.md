# Fixed Rate Doesn't Mean What You Think It Means

## Hook
Getting an ARM because you don't think you'll stay long makes total sense. And that's exactly why people end up stuck with a payment they can't afford.

## Branding
I've helped people refinance out of an ARM right before it reset — here's what happened.

## Problem
I had a client come to me. Years earlier, they bought with an ARM. Made sense at the time — they didn't think they'd stay in that house very long.

## Empathy
But life happened. They stayed. And then their adjustment period hit.

## Answer
Their rate was about to jump to 9%.

VISUAL: text overlay — "Before: manageable rate → After: 9%"

That's not a typo. Nine percent. They could not afford that payment. So we moved fast, and refinanced them into a fixed rate before that adjustment kicked in.

## Change
An ARM isn't a bad loan. It can make total sense — for the right situation, for the right timeline. But your plans can change. And if they do, that adjustment date doesn't care what your plans turned into.

VISUAL: text card — "Do you know your ARM's adjustment date?" (arm-adjustment-date-card.png)

If you have an ARM right now — go find your adjustment date. Know it. Write it down. And if your plans have changed since you got that loan — start that refinance conversation early. Not the month before it adjusts.

## End Result
No surprise payment change down the road.

## CTA
Save this if you're not 100% sure what kind of rate you actually have.
