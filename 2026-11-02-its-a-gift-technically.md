## Part 1

*[Tasha calls Jordan, excited.]*  
**TASHA:** Jordan! Okay, good news. Huge news.  
**JORDAN:** I love huge news. What's going on?  
**TASHA:** My parents are giving us the down payment. Twenty thousand dollars.  
**JORDAN:** Tasha, that's amazing. That changes everything.  
**TASHA:** Right?! So what do you need from me?  
**JORDAN:** Easy. I need a gift letter. Your parents sign it. It says how much they're giving, where it's coming from, and that it's a gift. They're not expecting it back.  
**TASHA:** Totally. My dad's actually right here. Hold on, I'll put him on speaker.  
**DAD:** Hey, Jordan! I've heard a lot about you.  
**JORDAN:** Hi! Thank you for doing this. That's a really generous gift.  
**DAD:** Oh, happy to help. That's what family's for.  
**JORDAN:** Love that. So I'll send the gift letter over today. Just one signature, and we're good to go.  
**DAD:** Sure, sure. Send it over.  
**JORDAN:** Perfect. And it just confirms there's no repayment, so...  
**DAD:** Yep. And then she starts paying us back in January.  
**JORDAN:** I'm sorry?  
**DAD:** Six hundred a month. We worked it all out. Right, Tash?  
**TASHA:** *(quietly)* Is that a problem?  
**JORDAN:** *(long pause)* Okay. I need everybody to hear me for a second.  

## Part 2

*[Recap text on screen: Tasha's parents are "gifting" her $20,000 for the down payment… with $600 a month in repayments.]*  
**JORDAN:** Okay. Everybody take a breath. Nobody's in trouble. But I need to explain something.  
**DAD:** Did I say something wrong?  
**JORDAN:** Kind of. If you expect her to pay you back, that's not a gift. That's a loan.  
**TASHA:** But it's from my dad. It's family.  
**JORDAN:** I know. And it's really generous. But if you sign a letter that says "gift" when it's really a loan, that's not a paperwork thing. That's mortgage fraud.  
**DAD:** Whoa. Fraud? It's my own money.  
**JORDAN:** It is. But the lender is approving Tasha based on what she owes every month. A six-hundred-dollar payment to you is a real debt. It changes what she can afford.  
**TASHA:** So what do we do? Do we lose the house?  
**JORDAN:** No. We have two honest options. One: it's a true gift. No repayment, no strings. If she wants to help you out later, that's her choice. Not a bill.  
**DAD:** And two?  
**JORDAN:** Two: we call it what it is. A loan. We write it up, count the six hundred in her monthly debts, and see if she still qualifies.  
**TASHA:** And if I don't?  
**JORDAN:** Then we adjust your price range. Better to find out now than after you sign.  
**DAD:** *(pause)* Then it's a gift. No strings. She's my kid.  
**TASHA:** Dad.  
**DAD:** I mean it. Don't you dare send me six hundred dollars.  
**JORDAN:** *(laughs)* Love that. Sending the letter now.  

## Captions

**Caption 1:** Part 1 — Her parents were giving her $20,000 for the down payment. Then Dad said one sentence on speakerphone that changed everything. A gift letter has one job: confirming the money doesn't have to be paid back. Would you have caught this? 👇

**Caption 2:** Part 2 — If you have to pay it back, it's not a gift. It's a loan, and signing a gift letter that says otherwise is mortgage fraud. The honest fix is either a true no-strings gift, or counting it as a loan and making sure you still qualify. Has family ever "gifted" you money with strings attached? 👇
