# Cheap Money and Leverage Don't Come Together

**Type:** Talking Head
**Status:** Scripted
**Date approved:** 2026-09-21
**CTA:** Discussion question
**Image asset:** None
**Need:** Significance + Variety

*Illustrative client story.* **Source:** [Redfin, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.redfin.com/news/contract-cancellations-july-2026/) (51% more sellers than buyers in July). Re-angle of #3 and #69 — same problem, new take.

---

## Hook (Misconception Flip)

Waiting for rates to drop is the smart move.

Right up until everyone else has the same idea.

## Branding

If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Proof

I had a client who waited for a better rate.

When rates finally dipped, so did her leverage.

Suddenly, every house she liked had multiple offers.

## Distinction

I'm not saying buy at any price.

I'm saying compare the two situations honestly.

## Answer

Right now, Redfin says there are 51 percent more sellers than buyers.

That's negotiating room.

When rates fall, buyers usually come back, and that room shrinks.

VISUAL: Two-column card, "Now: higher rates, sellers negotiating / Later: lower rates, more buyers"

## Change

Run your real numbers both ways, with a price cut, or a seller-paid buydown, or a lower rate later.

## Stance

Here's my take.

If I had to pick one, I'd pick the leverage.

## End Result

You can refinance a rate.

You can't renegotiate a price after you close.

## CTA (Discussion question)

Are you waiting for rates or shopping now?

I want to know why, because it tells me how buyers are really thinking.
