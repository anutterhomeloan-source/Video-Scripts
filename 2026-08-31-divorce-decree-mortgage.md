## Hook
Your divorce decree says the house is yours. Your mortgage lender never got that memo.

## Branding
I've walked a lot of people through this exact situation since COVID, so I know where it goes wrong.

## Problem
I saw this constantly coming out of COVID. I still see it today. Someone gets divorced. The decree says: "She keeps the house." Or: "He keeps the house." And they think that's it. Done. Settled.

## Empathy
But a decree is a legal document between two people. It's not a mortgage document.

VISUAL: text overlay — "Decree ≠ Off the loan"

## Answer
Your ex's name doesn't come off that loan just because a judge said so. Which means their credit is still tied to it. Their debt-to-income is still tied to it. If a payment gets missed, it hits both of you.

## Change
The person keeping the house needs to refinance the loan into their name only. Or, in some cases, do a loan assumption if the lender allows it. That's the only way to actually separate it.

VISUAL: checklist — "To actually separate the loan: refinance into one name, or assumption if allowed" (divorce-mortgage-checklist-card.png)

And the sooner you start that process after the divorce is final, the better. Don't wait until you're trying to sell, or refinance for something else, and find out it was never actually handled.

## End Result
One name on the loan, one person's credit on the line — no surprises later.
