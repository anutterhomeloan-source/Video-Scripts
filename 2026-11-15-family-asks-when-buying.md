## Hook

If somebody at Thanksgiving asks you when you're finally going to buy a house, I want you to have this answer ready.

The typical first-time buyer in America is now forty years old.

> VISUAL: NAR article screenshot, highlight "Median First-Time Buyer Age Is Still 40"


## Shame removal

So if you're thirty-four and still renting, you are not behind. And that's not because you did something wrong. Prices went up, rates went up, and the timeline changed for everybody.

And by the way, if you don't know who I am, I'm Jordan Nutter, I'm a loan officer here in Atlanta, and I help make mortgages make sense.


## Validation + receipts

And honestly, if you've been saving, working on your credit, and not rushing into something you can't afford, that's not behind. That's exactly what you're supposed to be doing.

This is from the National Association of Realtors' new buyer report. Forty is the median age for first-time buyers, the same record high as last year. And first-time buyers who financed their home were a little younger, around thirty-eight.

> [Optional real client line: "I just had a client who…" (your own example lands harder).]


## Objection kill + change

And if you're thinking, well, I don't have twenty percent down, most first-time buyers don't. There are loans that let you put down a lot less than that.

So here's what I'd actually do. Don't wait until you feel ready. Find out your real number: what you'd qualify for, what the payment would be, and what you'd need saved. Then you have a plan instead of a guess.


## Stance + CTA

So here's my take. Buying at forty with a plan beats buying at twenty-five and being house-poor.

So if you want to know your real number before the next family dinner, the link is in my bio, and we'll figure it out together.
