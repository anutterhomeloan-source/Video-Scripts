*Based on a real Florida closing: after a hurricane and state of emergency, no properties could close until re-inspected. House was fine; buyer and seller weren't happy. Characters: Jordan, Buyer, Seller's Agent (phone).*

---

## Part 1

*[Buyer calls Jordan. Borrowed room, boxes in background.]*

**BUYER:** Jordan! Hey. So the hurricane's gone. We're still closing Thursday, right?

**JORDAN:** Okay. Hi. First of all, are you safe? Is everybody okay?

**BUYER:** Yeah, we're fine. We're at my sister's. But we're good to close?

**JORDAN:** So. About that. The governor declared a state of emergency.

**BUYER:** Right. For the storm.

**JORDAN:** Which means before any loan in the area can close, the house has to be re-inspected.

**BUYER:** Re-inspected? It passed inspection. Three weeks ago.

**JORDAN:** It passed inspection before a hurricane went over it.

**BUYER:** It's fine! I drove by. Well, I didn't drive by. The roads are closed. But my agent's cousin said it looks fine.

**JORDAN:** Your agent's cousin.

**BUYER:** He has a drone.

**JORDAN:** I love that for him. But the lender needs a real inspector to confirm the house is in the same condition it was in when it appraised. That house is what's backing your loan.

**BUYER:** Okay. So how long does that take?

**JORDAN:** Well. Every house in the area needs one. So there's a line.

**BUYER:** How long of a line?

**JORDAN:** I don't know yet.

**BUYER:** Jordan. My lease ends Saturday. The movers are booked. The sellers already moved out of state!

*[Phone buzzes.]*

**BUYER:** Oh no. It's the seller's agent.

**JORDAN:** Put her on.

**SELLER'S AGENT:** Hi, yeah. My sellers want to know why we're not closing Thursday. And they are not happy. They said if we don't close this week, they're calling their attorney.

**JORDAN:** *(deep breath)* Okay. Here's what's happening.

## Part 2

*[TEXT ON SCREEN: Part 2]*

**BUYER:** So the hurricane hit, every house needs a re-inspection, and now the sellers are calling their attorney. Great.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Breathe. Let's walk through it.

**SELLER'S AGENT:** My sellers already moved. They're paying a mortgage on a house they don't even live in. Every day costs them money.

**JORDAN:** I hear you. And honestly, I'm not happy about it either. But this isn't about your buyer. Nothing in this area closes until it's re-inspected. It's every closing.

**SELLER'S AGENT:** So what do we do?

**JORDAN:** The re-inspection is ordered. The minute it comes back clean, we close. I'll update everyone every single day.

**BUYER:** And my movers?

**JORDAN:** Let me ask you something. Where are your movers right now?

**BUYER:** I mean, probably at home.

**JORDAN:** Because?

**BUYER:** Because the roads are closed.

**JORDAN:** Right. So even if we closed Thursday—

**BUYER:** —nobody could drive to the house.

**JORDAN:** Nobody's moving into anything Thursday. The storm moved your closing. It didn't take your house.

*[TEXT ON SCREEN: 6 days later]*

**JORDAN:** Hey. Re-inspection report's in.

**BUYER:** And?

**JORDAN:** Not a shingle out of place.

**BUYER:** So it was fine the whole time?

**JORDAN:** It was fine the whole time. But now we know it, and so does your lender. We're closing Tuesday.

**BUYER:** Can I say something?

**JORDAN:** Sure.

**BUYER:** Tell the agent's cousin his drone was right.

## Captions (approved, one per part)
Part 1 — The hurricane passed, the house looked fine, and they were closing Thursday. Then the state of emergency hit. When a disaster is declared after the appraisal, lenders need the home re-inspected before closing, because that house is what's backing the loan. And every house in the area is in the same line. Would you have been as calm as I was?

Part 2 — If you're buying in a storm-prone area, build a little cushion into your closing date and your move. A re-inspection after a declared disaster isn't your lender being difficult. It protects you and the house you're about to own. How much wiggle room do you give your closing date?
