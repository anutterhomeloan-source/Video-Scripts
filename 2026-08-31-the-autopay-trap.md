# The Autopay Trap

- **Type:** Reenactment
- **Status:** Scripted
- **Date approved:** 2026-08-31
- **CTA:** None
- **Image asset:** None
- **Need:** Certainty

*Based on a real client scenario, fictionalized (names and identifying details changed). Characters: Jordan (loan officer), Diane (buyer — grown kids, wants to buy her own place; cosigned car loans for all 3 kids, plus her own car, all four on her credit).*

---

## Part 1

**DIANE:** Jordan, I am so excited. Kids are finally grown, out of the house. I want something that's just mine.

**JORDAN:** I love that for you. Let's get your credit pulled and see where we're at.

*(pause — credit report pulling)*

**JORDAN:** Okay… Diane, I'm seeing four auto loans on here.

**DIANE:** Oh, those aren't really mine.

**JORDAN:** They're reporting under your name.

**DIANE:** Right, but one's my own car. The other three — I cosigned for my kids. They pay for those, not me.

**JORDAN:** Even so — if they're on your credit, they count against you unless we can document someone else is actually paying them.

**DIANE:** Well, I mean… technically I pay them.

**JORDAN:** Wait — what do you mean, technically?

**DIANE:** I have it set up on autopay. From my account. Just in case one of them ever forgets, or can't make it that month.

**JORDAN:** Diane… has that ever actually happened?

**DIANE:** No! Never. Not once. They always give me the money first.

**JORDAN:** Okay. I need you to sit down for a second, because that detail is about to matter a lot more than you think.

## Part 2

**JORDAN:** Here's the problem. To leave a cosigned loan off your debt-to-income ratio, we need twelve months of proof the other person paid it — from their own account. Not yours.

**DIANE:** But they DO pay it. They just give me the money, and I pay the loan.

**JORDAN:** I believe you. But on paper, that's you paying a car payment every single month. Three of them, plus your own.

**DIANE:** So all four count against me?

**JORDAN:** All four. And that changes your numbers a lot.

**DIANE:** I had no idea. I thought since they never missed one, it wouldn't matter.

**JORDAN:** I know. Most people think that. It's not about whether the payment gets made — it's about whose account it comes out of.

**DIANE:** So what do I do? I've been planning this for two years.

**JORDAN:** Here's the good news — this is fixable. Each of your kids sets up their own autopay, from their own account, starting today.

**DIANE:** And then?

**JORDAN:** Twelve months of statements showing they paid it themselves, and we can exclude those loans completely. Your numbers will look completely different.

**DIANE:** So I'm not stuck. I just have to wait it out.

**JORDAN:** You're not stuck. You're twelve months and three phone calls away. Let's put it on the calendar right now.

## CAPTIONS
- Part 1 (approved): She thought cosigning for her kids' cars would never come back to bite her. Four auto loans under your name — even if someone else hands you the money for them first — still count on your credit report. And when you go to buy your own home, that math doesn't care who wrote the check first. This is one of the most common surprises I see with parents who cosigned years ago and forgot about it.
- Part 2 (approved): Part 2 — the fix nobody explains. Cosigned debt isn't permanent on your application, but getting it removed takes real proof, not just trust. If you've cosigned for someone, this is exactly what your file needs to look like twelve months from now to get that loan off your numbers.
