## Hook

When you get a mortgage, you pay the company that gave you the loan for the next 30 years.
…Except a lot of people don't.
And the letter that tells you could end up in your junk pile.
I'm not talking about anything shady.
I'm talking about how the mortgage world actually works.
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

After you close, a lot of loans get sold to big investors.
And the company you send your payment to can change too.
That company is called your servicer.
VISUAL: Simple flow: "You → Lender → Investor," with "Servicer (who you pay)" labeled

## Empathy

Most people find out when a random letter shows up.
And the first thought is, "Wait. Is this a scam?"
Totally fair question.

## Answer

Here's the good news.
Your rate doesn't change.
Your payment doesn't change.
Your terms don't change.
Same loan. New mailing address.
VISUAL: Text call-out "Same loan. New address."

## Change

Here's what to watch for.
You'll get a letter from your old company saying goodbye.
And one from the new company saying hello.
Both should tell you who the new company is and the date it starts.
VISUAL: Two envelopes: "Goodbye letter" and "Hello letter"
And if you accidentally pay the old company?
For the first 60 days, you can't be charged a late fee for that.
Now, if someone calls or emails saying, "Send your payment here now,"
and you don't have both letters?
Don't send anything.
Call the number on your current statement first.

## Stance

Here's my take.
The person who helps you get the loan matters way more than the logo on your monthly bill.
So pick that person carefully.

## Caption (approved)
If you get a letter saying your mortgage is moving to a new company, don't panic. Loans often get sold after closing, and the company you pay can change. Your rate, payment, and terms stay the same. You should get a goodbye letter from the old company and a hello letter from the new one. If someone asks you to send payments somewhere new and you don't have both letters, call the number on your current statement first. Save this for later.
