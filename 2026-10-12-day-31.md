*Based on a real client story from one of Jordan's LOs, fictionalized (names changed). Soon-to-be-ex-wife has paid the mortgage 31 days late (first day it reports) for 13 straight months; client didn't know, credit tanked; his 94-year-old grandpa offers to co-sign with the girlfriend. Open ending on purpose. Characters (Jordan plays all): Jordan, Travis (client), Kelsey (girlfriend), Grandpa Earl (94, on speaker, sharp and funny). Jordan may tweak lines on film day. Note: overlaps the credit-pull-reveal beat of #59 Autopay Trap / #6 Co-Signed Secret by Jordan's choice; the day-31 pattern and grandpa co-signer are the new mechanics.*

## Part 1


*[Jordan at the desk, credit report on screen. Travis and Kelsey on a video call.]*

**JORDAN:** Okay, Travis. I've got your credit pulled.

**TRAVIS:** Great. We're ready. Kelsey already picked out curtains.

**KELSEY:** They're sage green.

**JORDAN:** Love that. Quick question. Who pays the mortgage on your current house?

**TRAVIS:** My wife. Well. Soon-to-be-ex-wife. She's always done the bills.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Because that mortgage has been paid late.

**TRAVIS:** Late? Like, one time?

**JORDAN:** Thirteen times.

**TRAVIS:** What?

**JORDAN:** Thirteen months in a row. And here's the weird part. Every single payment is 31 days late. Not 29. Not 45. Thirty-one.

**KELSEY:** Why is that weird?

**JORDAN:** Because a payment doesn't show up as late on your credit until it's 30 days past due. So day 31 is the very first day it can hurt you.

**TRAVIS:** So she's paying it…

**JORDAN:** Every month. Right after it counts.

**TRAVIS:** (long stare) She knows exactly what she's doing.

**KELSEY:** Can't we just dispute it?

**JORDAN:** They're real late payments. They're accurate. They stay on there for seven years. And right now, with this credit, Travis can't qualify for the new house.

**KELSEY:** Okay. Okay. Then we'll just use Grandpa.

**JORDAN:** Use… Grandpa?

**TRAVIS:** My grandpa. He's 94. Perfect credit.

**KELSEY:** He's actually on the call. Grandpa Earl?

**GRANDPA EARL:** (way too loud) I'M HERE. WHO'S JORDAN?

## Part 2


*[TEXT ON SCREEN: Part 2]*

**JORDAN:** Hi, Earl! I'm the loan officer.

**GRANDPA EARL:** Good. I hear my grandson's ex pays the bills on day 31.

**TRAVIS:** Grandpa.

**GRANDPA EARL:** I paid off my house in 1971. Paid cash for the truck. Never been late on anything.

**JORDAN:** Earl, I wish all my clients had your credit.

**GRANDPA EARL:** Everybody's credit is better than his.

**JORDAN:** So here's what I need you to hear. If you co-sign with Kelsey, you're not a reference. You're a borrower.

**GRANDPA EARL:** Meaning?

**JORDAN:** Meaning if a payment is late, it's late on your credit too. Your name is on that loan for as long as it exists.

**GRANDPA EARL:** Young lady, I've been on the hook for this boy since he was sixteen.

**JORDAN:** And Travis. You're not on this loan.

**TRAVIS:** Right. Because of my credit.

**JORDAN:** Which means this house is Kelsey's and Earl's. Not yours.

**TRAVIS:** Wait. What?

**KELSEY:** Babe. It's fine.

**JORDAN:** And your old mortgage? Your name's still on it. Divorce or no divorce, until that loan is refinanced or the house is sold, every day-31 payment keeps hitting your credit.

**TRAVIS:** So what do I do?

**JORDAN:** Today? Call the company you pay, get your own login, and make sure that payment goes out on time yourself.

**GRANDPA EARL:** Jordan. Give me a minute. I need to talk to my grandson.

**TRAVIS:** Uh oh.


*[TEXT ON SCREEN: Would you let Grandpa co-sign?]*

## Captions (approved)
Part 1 — He thought his soon-to-be-ex was paying the mortgage. She was. Just on day 31, every month, for 13 months. A late payment doesn't hit your credit until it's 30 days past due, so day 31 is the first day it counts. If someone else handles a bill with your name on it, check your credit yourself. Would you have caught this?

Part 2 — Co-signing isn't vouching for someone. It's taking on the loan. If payments are late, it's on the co-signer's credit too, for as long as the loan exists. And even after a divorce, your name stays on the old mortgage until it's refinanced or the house is sold. Would you let Grandpa co-sign?
