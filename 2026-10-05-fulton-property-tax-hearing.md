## Hook

I'm a loan officer in Atlanta.
And if you own a home in Fulton County, there's a meeting Wednesday morning that could change your mortgage payment.
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

Fulton County just proposed a property tax rate that's higher than what's called the rollback rate.
And I know. Nobody reads those notices.
VISUAL: Screenshot of the press release, "higher than the rollback rate" and hearing dates highlighted


## Empathy

And you're not supposed to know what a rollback rate is.
Nobody teaches you this when you buy a house.

## Answer

So here's the simple version.
When home values go up, the county reassesses your house.
The rollback rate is the tax rate that would bring in the same money as last year, even with those higher values.
Anything above that? Georgia treats it as a tax increase.
That's why the county has to hold public hearings first.
Now here's where your mortgage comes in.
If you have an escrow account, your property taxes are built into your monthly payment.
So when your tax bill goes up, your lender re-runs your escrow.
And your payment goes up with it.
Same loan. Same rate. Higher payment.
VISUAL: Payment breakdown bar (principal & interest + taxes + insurance), tax slice growing


## Change

So here's what to know.
One. The last public hearing is this Wednesday, October 7th, at 10 a.m.
It's at the Fulton County Government Center on Pryor Street, and you can join on Zoom.
If you want to be heard, that's where.
Two. This is just the county's piece of your bill.
Your city and your school district set their own rates.
Three. When your next assessment notice comes in the mail, actually open it.
In Georgia, you have 45 days from that notice to appeal if the value looks wrong.

## Stance

And here's my take.
Most people will argue with their lender over a tiny difference in rate.
But they'll never look at a tax bill that costs them more every single year.
