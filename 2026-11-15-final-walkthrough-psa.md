## Hook

A buyer at my company showed up for their final walkthrough an hour before closing, and the house was flooded.

That house did not close.

And if you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter and I help make mortgages make sense.


## Problem + Empathy

A lot of people treat the final walkthrough like a formality. You've had the inspection, you've seen the house five times, and you just want the keys.

And I get it. By closing day, you're exhausted.


## Answer

But the walkthrough is your last chance to make sure the house is in the condition you agreed to buy it in. Because once you sign, it's yours, and so is anything wrong with it.


## Change

So here's what I'd check.

First, run every faucet and flush every toilet, and look under the sinks while the water's running.

Second, look up at the ceilings for stains or drips.

Third, turn on the heat and the air, and test any appliances that are staying.

And fourth, make sure anything the seller agreed to fix actually got fixed, and ask for the receipts.

> VISUAL: Checklist overlay filling in as each item is said: FAUCETS + UNDER SINKS / CEILINGS / HVAC + APPLIANCES / REPAIRS + RECEIPTS

And try to schedule it as close to closing as you can, like that same day or the day before.


## Stance + End Result + CTA

So here's my take. If your schedule is too tight for a walkthrough, your schedule is too tight.

Because that buyer was upset that day. But they would've been a lot more upset owning a flooded house. So save this for your closing day.
