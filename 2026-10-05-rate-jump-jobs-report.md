## Hook

Mortgage rates just had their biggest one-week jump in four years.
Then on Friday, we got a jobs report that should have brought them back down.
It didn't.
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

On Thursday, Freddie Mac said the average 30-year rate hit 7.28%.
That's up from 7.03% the week before.
Highest since 2023.
And by Friday, Mortgage News Daily had it at 7.57%.
VISUAL: Line chart: 7.03% → 7.28% (Freddie Mac, Oct 1) → 7.57% (Mortgage News Daily, Oct 2), source labels on screen


## Empathy

If you saw that and wanted to pause your whole house search, that's normal.
That's a scary week.

## Answer

But here's what most people get wrong.
Mortgage rates don't follow the Fed.
They follow the bond market, specifically the 10-year Treasury.
And right now, that's above 5%.
So Friday, the jobs report came in weak.
Only 29,000 jobs. Economists expected more than 80,000.
Normally? Weak jobs means bonds rally, and rates come down.
And they did, for a few hours.
Then bonds gave it all back, and lenders raised rates by the end of the day.
This morning, rates are a little lower again.
That's how fast this moves.
VISUAL: Headline citation: "29,000 jobs added in September" (Scotsman Guide, Oct 2)


## Change

So what do you actually do?
Don't plan around one week. Plan around a payment.
Have your lender run your numbers at today's rate, and half a point higher.
If you're under contract, ask about your lock, and whether a float-down is an option.
And if you're in Atlanta, the market has shifted.
Georgia MLS put it this way in August: homes are competing for buyers now.
That's room to negotiate.

## Stance

Here's my take.
If one bad week can scare you out of buying, you didn't have a plan.
You had a rate wish.

## End Result + CTA (Discussion question)

Know your payment, and next week's headline can't make the decision for you.
Now I want to know. Did this week change your plans? Pause, wait, or keep going?
Tell me in the comments. It honestly tells me a lot about what buyers are feeling right now.
