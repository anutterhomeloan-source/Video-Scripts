## Script

Getting approved for more...

doesn't mean you should spend more.

And that gap is exactly where buyers get into trouble.

This is step two of nine in Your Next Move.

VISUAL: Your Next Move board graphic, Step 2 (Set A Budget) highlighted — your-next-move-step2.png

I had a client last week. Purchase price: $300,000.

I ran her numbers before we even got on the phone. She qualified. Easily.

Then I asked her the first question I always ask. Not "how much do you want to spend." "What's your ideal monthly payment."

And the number she gave me didn't come close to what $300,000 actually costs a month.

She stops me. Says, "Wait — I already talked to another lender. They approved me for more than what you're telling me."

And I got it. I did. On paper, it wasn't even an issue. She qualified for that price, easy.

But I told her — qualifying on paper isn't the same as being comfortable every single month for the next thirty years.

Yes. Technically. That's not my money on the line.

But it feels like my money. Because you're trusting me with the biggest purchase of your life. So I'm going to do everything I can to make sure you're actually comfortable — even when that number doesn't match what you were hoping to hear.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about that approval letter — the number on it isn't a target. It's just the most a computer thinks you can survive paying.

Hitting it isn't the goal. Being comfortable is.

So step two isn't "get approved for as much as possible." It's sitting down and getting honest about the payment you actually want to live with — before you fall in love with a number some other lender handed you.

See you for step three — where I tell you what that approval letter actually means, and why most people get it wrong.
