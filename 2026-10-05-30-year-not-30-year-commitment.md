## Hook

If you looked at your loan estimate, saw the total interest over 30 years, and felt sick, good.
That means you're paying attention.
It's just not the number that matters most.
If you don't know who I am, my name is Jordan Nutter, and I help make mortgages make sense.

## Problem

Your loan estimate shows what you'd pay in interest if you kept that loan all 30 years.
Every payment. Never moving. Never refinancing.
And on a $400,000 loan at 7%?
That's about $558,000 in interest.
VISUAL: "$558,000 in interest" big on screen, then a red question mark


## Empathy

That number is supposed to make you think.
But it scares a lot of people out of buying altogether.
And I get it.

## Answer

But here's the thing.
Thirty years is the longest the loan can last.
It's not how long most people keep it.
According to the National Association of Realtors, the typical seller had owned their home for 11 years.
And that's a record high.
Eleven. Not thirty.
People sell. They move. They refinance if rates allow.
VISUAL: Timeline bar 0–30 years, marker at "11 years (NAR record high)"

I've owned three homes.
And I'll be honest, I still have the mortgage on my first one.
It's an FHA loan, and the rate is so low, I'm not letting it go.
That's the point.
I chose to keep it.
The 30 years gave me options. It didn't lock me in.

## Change

So flip to page 3 of your loan estimate.
There's a section called "In 5 Years."
It shows what you'll actually have paid, and how much of your loan you'll have paid down, in the first five years.
For a lot of people, that's a much more honest picture.
Then ask yourself: how long will I realistically live here?
VISUAL: Mock of the Loan Estimate "Comparisons" section with "In 5 Years" circled


## Stance

Here's the hill I'll die on.
Stop judging your mortgage by year 30.
Judge it by the years you'll actually live there.

## End Result + CTA (Leave a comment)

Do that, and a scary number stops making the decision for you.
So tell me in the comments: how long do you plan to stay in your next home?
Five years? Ten? Forever?
