# The First Client I Almost Lost

**Type:** Talking Head
**Status:** Scripted
**Date approved:** 2026-09-14
**CTA:** Leave a comment
**Image asset:** None

---

**Hook**
Early in my career, I almost lost a client. And it wasn't because of their credit. It was because of me.

**Branding**
If you don't know who I am, I'm Jordan Nutter. I've been doing this long enough now to know exactly where new loan officers go wrong — because I made the mistake myself.

**Problem**
Something came up on their file. I thought I could fix it quietly. On my own. Before anyone had to worry.

**Empathy**
If you're new to this business, you know that instinct. You want to be the one who handles it. You don't want to be the one delivering bad news.

VISUAL: Text overlay — "the call I'll never forget" at the story's turn

**Answer**
So I sat on it. I tried to fix it myself. And by the time I told my client — and their agent — it was too late. We couldn't close.

**Change**
Now, the second something shifts on a file — even before I have the full picture — my client hears from me first. Not last. Because a delayed update costs people so much more than an early "hey, something came up" ever will.

VISUAL: Before/after overlay — "Old habit: wait until I have the full picture" vs. "Now: tell them the second something shifts"

**End Result**
That mistake is the reason I communicate the way I do now. And it's the reason my clients trust me with the hard news, not just the good news.

**CTA**
If you're in this business — or you've ever been on the other side of a bad update — tell me below.

## Caption

Caption 1:
The first client I almost lost taught me the one rule I never break now. Communication in this business isn't about having all the answers — it's about not going silent when something's uncertain. If your loan officer goes quiet for more than a few days, that's not a good sign. Ask what's happening, even if the honest answer is "still figuring it out."
