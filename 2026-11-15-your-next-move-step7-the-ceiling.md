## Script

So your pre-approval letter says you're approved up to a certain number.

And most people think that's the number you're supposed to show the seller.

It's not.

This is step seven of nine in Your Next Move.

VISUAL: Your Next Move board graphic, Step 7 (The Ceiling) highlighted — your-next-move-step7.png

So here's the thing. That number on your letter is your ceiling. It's the most a lender will let you borrow. It's not your target, and it's definitely not something you want to hand the other side of the deal.

Because think about it. Let's say you're offering three sixty on a house, and you send a letter that says you're approved for four hundred.

The seller and their agent can read that letter. And now they know you've got forty thousand dollars of room.

So when they counter, guess where they're going to aim?

I've watched buyers lose their negotiating power before the negotiation even started, just because of what was on that letter.

So here's what I tell my clients. When you write an offer, ask your loan officer for a letter that matches that offer. Same price, same down payment. It still proves you're qualified, but it doesn't show your whole hand.

A good loan officer will turn that around for you quickly, even on a weekend.

So your letter proves you can buy the house. It shouldn't tell them how much more you could pay.

That's step seven. Step eight is next, and it's all about what you can actually ask for, because the price is only one piece of it.
