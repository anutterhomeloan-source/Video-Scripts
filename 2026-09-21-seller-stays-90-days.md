# The Seller Wants to Stay 90 Days

**Type:** Reenactment
**Status:** Scripted
**Date approved:** 2026-09-21
**CTA:** None
**Image asset:** None
**Need:** Certainty + Contribution

*Illustrative scenario (not a specific real client). Characters: Jordan (loan officer), Priya (buyer who agreed to a long seller rent-back). Mechanic: a paperwork/legal twist — occupancy rules on a primary-residence loan.*

⚠️ **Before filming:** conventional/Fannie Mae expects occupancy within 60 days of closing and at least one year ([Selling Guide B2-1.1-01](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.1-01/occupancy-types)). Confirm NFM's policy and any exception process.

---

## Part 1

**PRIYA:** Jordan, great news. The seller said yes.

**JORDAN:** Yes to what?

**PRIYA:** He asked to stay in the house for ninety days after closing. I said sure!

**JORDAN:** Ninety days.

**PRIYA:** It's a nice thing to do. His new build isn't ready.

**JORDAN:** That is kind.

**PRIYA:** Right? I felt like a good person.

**JORDAN:** Where are you living those ninety days?

**PRIYA:** My apartment. I'll just extend my lease.

**JORDAN:** And when do you plan to move in?

**PRIYA:** After he moves out. January.

**JORDAN:** Okay. Deep breath.

**PRIYA:** Why are you doing that?

**JORDAN:** Because this loan is for your primary residence.

**PRIYA:** It's going to be my primary residence!

**JORDAN:** Eventually. The loan expects you to move in within sixty days of closing.

**PRIYA:** Sixty? He's staying ninety.

**JORDAN:** I know.

**PRIYA:** So what does that mean?

**JORDAN:** A favor you already signed may put your loan at risk.

**PRIYA:** I signed it yesterday.

**JORDAN:** Of course you did.

**PRIYA:** Was that wrong?

**JORDAN:** It wasn't wrong. It just wasn't checked.

**PRIYA:** I already told my landlord I'd stay through January!

**JORDAN:** Okay. Stay calm. We'll sort it out.

## Part 2

**JORDAN:** Nobody's in trouble yet. Let's fix it.

**PRIYA:** Please. What do we do?

**JORDAN:** On this loan, you move in within sixty days and live there at least a year.

**PRIYA:** So ninety days is a no.

**JORDAN:** Ninety days is a no.

**PRIYA:** Sixty days feels fast.

**JORDAN:** It's a standard timeline for a primary residence.

**PRIYA:** But he has plans! What if he says no?

**JORDAN:** Then we ask what he actually needs. Ninety days, or just a place to be?

**PRIYA:** I don't know.

**JORDAN:** Neither do I. That's why we ask.

**JORDAN:** Let's find out. Your agent asks for forty-five days, in writing, with a short rent-back agreement.

**PRIYA:** And if he can't?

**JORDAN:** Then we see if there's an exception. I'm not promising one.

**PRIYA:** That's not very comforting.

**JORDAN:** I'd rather be honest now than surprised at closing.

*(phone buzzes)*

**PRIYA:** It's my agent. Seller says forty-five days works. His builder finished early.

**JORDAN:** There it is.

**PRIYA:** So I almost lost the house over being nice.

**JORDAN:** You caught it before closing.

**PRIYA:** Next time I call you before I say yes to anything.

**JORDAN:** That's all I ask.

---

## Captions

Part 1 — She said yes to a favor, and it could have cost her the loan. Her seller asked to stay 90 days after closing, but a primary-residence loan expects you to move in within about 60. Before you agree to a rent-back, check it against your loan.

Part 2 — The fix was a shorter rent-back, in writing. If a favor changes your closing timeline, call your loan officer before you sign. It's a five-minute call.
