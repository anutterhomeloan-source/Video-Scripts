# Fannie Mae Just Killed the Lease Requirement for Landlords-to-Be

---

**Real story:** Fannie Mae restructured "departing residence" rental income rules. Old rule: executed lease required, 75% of gross rent counted, borrowers without 12 months of landlord experience could only use rent to offset the departing home's payment, not generate qualifying income. New rule: market-supported rent (an appraiser's assessment) replaces the lease requirement, reserve requirements are built into the calculation, and the 12-month landlord-experience offset limitation persists within the new framework. Mandatory for applications dated November 1, 2026+; lenders may adopt immediately.

**Hook**
If you want to buy your next house and turn your current one into a rental, you need a signed tenant in place first — before you can even qualify. That's what everyone still believes. And that rule just quietly disappeared.

**Distinction**
I'm not talking about ignoring the numbers here — reserve requirements still apply, and if you don't have twelve months of landlord experience, there are still limitations on how that income counts. I'm talking about the actual chicken-and-egg problem that used to stop people before they even started. You couldn't get a tenant without owning the second property. And you couldn't qualify for the second property without a tenant.

**Answer**
That's gone now. Instead, lenders can use an appraiser's assessment of what that home could rent for on the market — no lease required.

**Change**
This goes fully into effect November first, but lenders can start using it right now.
